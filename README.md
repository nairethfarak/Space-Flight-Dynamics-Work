# Space-Flight-Dynamics-Work
This repository contains a collection of academic projects and computational assignments developed for the Space Flight Dynamics course.

The projects use Python-based numerical methods and orbital mechanics theory to analyze spacecraft trajectories, orbital motion, rendezvous maneuvers, and interplanetary transfers.

**Main topics:**  
`Orbital Mechanics` · `Spacecraft Dynamics` · `Trajectory Analysis` · `Orbit Propagation` · `Rendezvous` · `Interplanetary Transfers`

## Projects
### Ballistic Trajectory
*File:* Ballistic_Trajectory

Analysis of the post-burnout trajectory of a rocket under the assumption that atmospheric drag can be neglected after engine shutdown.

The vehicle reaches burnout at an altitude of 5 km, with a flight path angle of 45° and a velocity of 2 km/s (approximately Mach 5.9). The equations of motion are numerically solved until the vehicle impacts the Earth's surface.

*Main tasks:*

- Numerical integration of the equations of motion.
- Determination of the ballistic trajectory after burnout.
- Calculation of total flight time.
- Calculation of the surface distance traveled until impact.
- 2D visualization of the trajectory and a simplified representation of the Earth's surface.

### Mars Rendezvous
*File:* Mars_Rendezvous

Numerical analysis of a rendezvous maneuver between a space station and a crewed spacecraft.

The maneuver begins 3 hours after the initial epoch, with the launch of a supply vehicle from the station's current position. A numerical search is used to determine the required departure velocity for the supply vehicle to reach the crewed spacecraft exactly 200 minutes later.

*Main tasks:*
- Numerical search for the required departure velocity.
- Propagation of the spacecraft trajectories.
- Analysis of the rendezvous and docking maneuver.
- Calculation of velocity changes ($\Delta v$) required for departure and docking.
- Determination of spacecraft positions and velocities at critical mission events.
- Calculation of classical orbital elements: Semi-major axis ($a$), Semilatus rectum ($p$), Periapsis ($q$), Eccentricity ($e$), Inclination ($I$), Argument of periapsis ($\omega$) and Orbital period ($T$).
- Visualization and animation of the rendezvous maneuver


### Interplanetary Mission

*File:* Interplanetary_Mission

Analysis of an interplanetary transfer and launch-window selection using a porkchop plot.

The porkchop diagram is generated by evaluating the required Earth departure and planetary arrival conditions over a range of possible dates. The characteristic energy,

$$
C_3 = v_\infty^2,
$$

is used as the primary metric to evaluate the energy required for departure from Earth.

*Main tasks:*

- Analysis of different Earth departure dates.
- Analysis of different planetary arrival dates.
- Calculation of the required hyperbolic excess velocity.
- Generation of a porkchop diagram.
- Identification of low-$C_3$ launch opportunities.
- Selection of an optimal launch window based on departure energy requirements.

### Space Flight Dynamics Problems
*File:* Space_Flight_Dynamics_Problems
A collection of computational problems covering fundamental concepts in space flight dynamics and orbital mechanics.

The notebook uses Python to calculate and analyze:

- Elliptical and hyperbolic orbits.
- Orbital velocity and orbital period.
- Eccentricity and other orbital parameters.
- Mean, eccentric, and true anomalies.
- Areas swept during orbital motion.
- Satellite position and velocity at different times.
- Transformations between perifocal and geocentric-equatorial reference frames.
- Classical orbital elements.
- Euler angles, including yaw, pitch, and roll.
- 2D and 3D representations of spacecraft orbits around Earth.
